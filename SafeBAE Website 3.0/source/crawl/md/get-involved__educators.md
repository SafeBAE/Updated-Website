# For Educators

## We know how very much educators are tasked with in any given day, so we have worked to provide tools for all levels of use. Start wherever you can — the most important part is helping your students feel engaged and invited into this work.

### Teaching Guides

Our Teaching Guides are built for short, meaningful discussions and can work well in advisory, health class, homeroom, clubs, or small-group settings. Each guide is paired with a youth-led educational video and includes discussion prompts and classroom activities.

[View Teaching Guides](https://www.safebae.org/resources/teaching-guides/ "Teaching Guides")

### Classroom Posters

Our Posters are easy-to-share educational materials that can help reinforce key messages around consent, healthy relationships, boundaries, and student support. They work well in classrooms, counseling offices, hallways, wellness centers, and student spaces.

[Download Posters](https://www.safebae.org/resources/posters/ "Posters")

### Consent Curriculum

Our Consent Curriculum is designed for deeper, more sustained engagement across multiple class periods. It covers consent, healthy relationships, bystander intervention, and response — with structured lesson plans that can be adapted for different school contexts.

[Learn More](https://www.safebae.org/360-schools/consent-curriculum/ "Learn More")

### SafeBAE 360° Schools

Our 360° Schools program is a comprehensive, survivor-centered model that builds lasting prevention infrastructure across your whole school community. It spans education, youth leadership, policy reform, and staff training — flexible enough to start small and grow over time.

[Learn More](https://www.safebae.org/360-schools/ "360° Schools")

#### Introducing Vibe Check

## **CheckYourVibe.org**

Vibe Check is a free, anonymous, self-guided tool that helps young people reflect on the health of their relationships. It is designed to be used privately and voluntarily — **it should never be assigned to students.** Making it available as an optional resource allows students who may need it to access it on their own terms, before you know anything specific about their situation.  
Consider sharing the link in your syllabus, on a resource wall, or anywhere students can find it without being asked to engage publicly.

[Explore the Tool](http://checkyourvibe.org "Vibe Check")
