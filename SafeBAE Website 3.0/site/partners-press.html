SafeBAE on The Longest Shortest Time

Preventing Teens from Joining the Manosphere

Episode page ↗Vibe Check →

SafeBAE returned to The Longest Shortest Time for a conversation with Hillary Frank about Vibe Check, SafeBAE’s free, anonymous reflection tool for young people who are worried they, or a friend, may have crossed a boundary or caused harm. Drew Davis, SafeBAE’s Director of Strategic Initiatives, talks about what happens when teens bring sensitive questions to Reddit, AI tools, or online spaces that can reinforce shame, denial, misogyny, or defensiveness, and how Vibe Check offers a different path toward reflection, accountability, and repair.

Shaping the national conversation

How to Not (Accidentally) Raise a Rapist

NYT Opinion piece ↗NYT podcast ↗

The first episode of the relaunch of Hillary Frank’s parenting and culture podcast reframes one of her most downloaded episodes, How to Not (Accidentally) Raise a Racist, to spark dialogue about raising young people who respect boundaries and safety. Featuring Shael Norris, it explores what works when talking to teens about consent and how schools and parents can build a consent-conscious culture.

Streaming now

Torrey DeVitto on Celebrity Name That Tune

Longtime SafeBAE partner and Board Member Torrey DeVitto competed on Celebrity Name That Tune (Season 5, Episode 12) on FOX and chose SafeBAE as the beneficiary of her winnings. Torrey donated $29,000 to support youth-led sexual violence prevention, bringing SafeBAE’s mission to a national television audience.

Her win reflects an ongoing commitment to our work and shows what trusted advocates can do when they use their platforms to invest directly in prevention, care, and youth leadership. We’re deeply grateful to Torrey for continuing to show up for SafeBAE, both publicly and behind the scenes.

Feature film partner

Disfluency

Disfluency is an award-winning feature film written and directed by Anna Baumgarten that offers a survivor-centered portrayal of the aftermath of sexual assault, focusing on trauma, memory, and recovery without depicting the assault itself.

SafeBAE partnered with the filmmakers to support the film’s educational impact, collaborating on school-based screenings and prevention and discussion resources for secondary and higher-education settings.

Training & education for professionals

We train the adults around young people, too

SafeBAE’s commitment to prevention extends beyond students. We regularly work with educators, legal advocates, service providers, parents, caregivers, and community leaders to share our youth-led model and equip adults to support survivors and embed prevention into everyday practice.

Through keynote presentations, workshops, and national webinars, we provide concrete strategies for engaging youth as leaders, building consent-conscious environments, and sustaining systemic change.
