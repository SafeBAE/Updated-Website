SafeBAE AI Literacy Training

Six modules for high school and college students on AI, how it works, where it can go wrong, and what it means for navigating relationships, consent, privacy, and a world increasingly shaped by algorithms.

Launches during U.S. Media Literacy Week, October 26–30, 2026

Start the training ↗Group access for educators ↗
Free6 modulesSelf-pacedProgress savesCertificate
Media Literacy Week, Oct 26–30

Who it’s for

Made for high school and college students

The training is designed for high school and college students, though anyone 13 or older may find it useful. It builds the skills to understand AI and how it affects your relationships, your safety and the decisions you make.

By the end, you will be able to:

  1. Think critically about AI-generated content, especially on sensitive topics like relationships, consent, and sexual health.
  2. Set healthy boundaries with AI tools and recognize signs of over-reliance.
  3. Understand AI’s limits, including what it can do, what it can’t do, and why it’s not a substitute for real-world connection.
  4. Know when to seek real-world support from trusted adults, friends, and resources.

What Is AI, Really?

Before we can talk about how AI affects our relationships, our thinking, or our safety, we need to understand what AI actually is and what it isn’t.

Traditional AI, machine learning, generative AI, hallucinations, and why privacy matters.

In this module, you will explore

  • What hallucinations are, and how to check a source yourself.
  • Why AI sounds correct even when it’s wrong.
  • Who can read your AI chats, and what to change.
  • What an AI agent is, and what changes when AI takes actions.
  • The Four-Step Check, a framework for using AI.

How AI Shapes the Way We Think

AI doesn’t just regurgitate information, it also shapes how we think.

Sycophancy, filter bubbles, emotional attachment, and the Four-Step Check skillset.

In this module, you will explore

  • How recommendation algorithms work and shape what you see online.
  • What information a feed collects about you, and what it has no way of knowing.
  • Social sycophancy and validation, or why AI tends to agree with you.
  • Confirmation bias and filter bubbles.
  • Emotional attachment and persuasion.
  • How to recognize online misinformation and manipulation.

Module 345 min8 screens

AI and Healthy Relationships

A chatbot can respond immediately, remember what you have told it and agree with how you feel. It is not in a relationship with you.

Simulated vs. genuine connection, AI companions, dependency, and boundaries.

In this module, you will explore

  • The difference between AI-simulated connection and genuine connection.
  • Emotional attachment to AI companions, and when reliance can become a problem.
  • Why companion apps are designed the way they are, and what laws and safety rules apply to them.
  • How AI can show up inside real relationships, and why that’s risky.
  • How AI can make you less receptive to boundaries, feedback and differing opinions.
  • What changes when an AI agent reads or sends messages that involve other people.

Nudify Apps and AI-Generated Image Abuse

This module focuses on one of the ways AI is being used to cause harm: creating sexual images of real people without their consent.

What nudification apps are, the harm and the law, real deepfake cases, and how to support someone who has been targeted.

Sextortion and Digital Manipulation

Sextortion is when someone threatens to share intimate images or information unless a person gives them something they want.

What sextortion is, how AI is making it more common and more dangerous, and how to protect yourself and support a friend.

In this module, you will explore

  • What sextortion is, and who does it.
  • The five stages it usually runs in, and what each one looks like.
  • How AI is making sextortion easier and more dangerous.
  • The warning signs of sextortion and manipulation.
  • Practical strategies for protecting yourself online.
  • How to support a friend who is going through sextortion.

Conclusion

Everything you learned, and where to go next.

What you’ll have learned, among other things

  • The Four-Step Check: Pause. Question. Check. Ask.
  • Why confident language is not evidence that an answer is right.
  • Why an AI can produce caring language without experiencing care.

A skill from Module 1

Pause. Question. Check. Ask.

The Four-Step Check gives you a simple way to decide what to trust, what to verify, and when an AI answer is not enough.

Keep going
aroundevery answer,
every time

1Step 1 of 4

Pause

Don’t accept the answer automatically.

Pause means creating a little space between getting an answer and believing or using it. AI can produce an answer instantly. That does not mean you need to accept it instantly.

ThenQuestionWhat is it claiming? How could you know if it is true?

2Step 2 of 4

Question

What is it claiming? How could you know if it is true?

Question means identifying what the AI is actually claiming, and what evidence would support it. Is it giving you a fact? A statistic? An interpretation? A guess about another person? Different claims need different evidence.

ThenCheckVerify important claims somewhere outside the AI.

3Step 3 of 4

Check

Verify important claims somewhere outside the AI.

Check means verifying important information somewhere outside the chatbot. If the AI gives you a citation, check that the source exists and says what the AI claims it says. The more important the answer is, the stronger your checking should be.

ThenAskFor anything about your life, ask a person.

4Step 4 of 4

Ask

For anything about your life, ask a person.

Ask means recognizing when information alone is not enough. Questions about your health, safety, relationships, school situation, legal rights, or another person’s intentions often depend on context an AI does not have.

Then back toPauseDon’t accept the answer automatically.

These four steps are for answers. An AI agent takes actions, so the check has to come before it acts. Module 2 builds on it.

How it works

Create an account and go at your own pace

  1. Create a free account

    Your name, your school or organization, a class code if someone gave you one, your email and a password. You confirm you are 13 or older.

  2. Go at your own pace

    Your progress is saved to your account, so you can stop any time and pick up where you left off on any phone or computer.

  3. Switch devices with a resume code

    Pick up on a phone, tablet or school computer. Your progress travels; anything you wrote stays on the device where you wrote it.

  4. Get your certificate

    A one-page certificate you can print or download. It unlocks once you have finished every screen and every activity.

Built into every screen

  • Quick ExitA Quick Exit button sits in the header of every screen.
  • SupportThe Support button lists free crisis lines and SafeBAE resources.
  • Text sizeSmall, Default, Large or Extra large.
  • FontHandwritten or Standard. Standard is a plainer font, for anyone who finds the handwritten one harder to read.
  • ActivitiesQuick checks, sorting activities, simulated feeds, scenarios and a personal AI boundaries plan.
  • Completion recordA printable record of which screens and activities were completed, and how much time was spent.

For parents and caregivers

The training is free and self-paced, for high school and college students and anyone 13 or older. When a learner joins with a class code, the teacher or group leader who gave it can see their name, school, email and how far they have got.

Learners can print or download a completion record to hand in when a teacher, parent, advisor or club leader asks for one.
